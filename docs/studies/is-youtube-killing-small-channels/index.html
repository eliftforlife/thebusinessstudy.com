Is YouTube Killing Small Channels? What the Data Says

2,100

By The Business Study · 2026-10-20

The short answer

No. For their size, small channels get more attention, not less. A channel with 5,000 subscribers typically got about 2,100 views a video, almost half its subscriber count. A channel with 5 million typically got about 360,000, only 7% of its subscribers. And 62% of small channels had a video hit 10 times their usual views, versus 29% of big ones.

01

Breakouts are more common when you’re small

Most small channels had at least one recent video that got 10 times their usual views. When we looked at why, it was usually the promise, and sometimes just luck.

What to do

Every video is another real chance. Small channels aren’t locked out; each video gets judged on its own promise.

02

Subscribers don’t guarantee views

Bigger channels got more views in total, but each video reached a smaller share of their audience.

What to do

Stop measuring a video by your subscriber count. Measure it by the promise you made.

How we did it

Channels with at least 10 recent videos from our channel set, grouped by subscriber count. Questions come from 23,660 question posts in six YouTube creator communities on Reddit (Oct 2025 to Oct 2026).

The limits

These channels were found through YouTube search, so they are findable channels, not every small channel. Plenty of small channels get almost no views.

Questions

Does YouTube push small channels?

For their size, yes. A typical video on a small channel got about 43 views for every 100 subscribers. On million-subscriber channels it was about 7 for every 100.

How much does subscriber count matter?

Less than you’d hope. More subscribers meant more total views, but a smaller share of them watched each video.
