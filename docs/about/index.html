About The Business Study.

We read the annual reports so you don't have to, and show how companies really make money: where it comes from, what they keep, and how they compare.

Where the numbers come from

Every company number on this site comes from that company's own annual report filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission: Form 10-K for U.S. companies, Form 20-F for a few foreign ones. We read the structured data (XBRL) inside each filing through the SEC's free EDGAR API, so the numbers are exactly what the company reported. Each page links to the filing it used.

Revenue breakdowns use the lines each company reports (products, services or segments). We pick lines that don't overlap, so the parts add up to the whole, and we say so when they don't. Employee counts are read from the "human capital" section of each report and checked by hand. "Kept of every $100" is net income divided by revenue.

The largest-company and most-profitable rankings use the SEC's "frames" data, which holds every U.S. public company's figures for the fiscal year closest to a calendar year.

What we don't do

We don't estimate, project or round numbers into something they aren't. If a filing doesn't report something (like a private company's revenue), it isn't here. Nothing on this site is investment advice.

Who runs it

The Business Study is a YouTube channel and website run by Elijah Westbrook. The short videos break down one company at a time; the narration is an AI voice. The site also hosts our earlier creator data studies on YouTube.

No company pays for coverage, and no company made or endorses this site. Logos are used only to identify the company a page is about.
